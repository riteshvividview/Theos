# Motion and signature refinement — 27 October 2026

The user requested more expressive desktop interactions, slower hero transitions, a reveal that cannot be triggered accidentally by a stationary cursor, and repair of depth-map tearing around the blue-clad woman's face. These instructions supersede the hover-to-reveal behavior documented in the earlier full-homepage revision.

## Changes

- Hero: desktop journey increased from 580vh to 820vh. Transition portion increased from 36% to 52% per world. At the same scroll speed this gives each transition approximately 2.17 times the prior scroll distance, while preserving a readable hold. Scene-navigation scrolling takes 2.8 seconds instead of 1.35. The title exit and shader hold timing follow the new transition boundary.
- Portrait integrity: the bedroom world uses a continuous projective image displacement rather than depth-dependent pixel offsets, both as an incoming and outgoing scene. This preserves the head, face, hair and clothing silhouette while retaining pointer perspective and cinematic camera movement. It remains image-based 2.5D, not reconstructed human geometry.
- Architectural scene (revised at the client's request): a 260vh full-bleed sequence with a 100vh sticky stage. On entry only the architectural study shows; the stage opens from an inset rounded frame to full bleed while the image settles from a slight zoom, then scroll pushes into the room through each layer's Magnific depth map (near is dark). The built interior (`Interiorimg.png`) was generated wider than the study, so the blueprint was regenerated from it: `architecture-plan.jpg` is the same room drawn as an architect's concept board, pixel-aligned with `Interiorimg.png`. Both layers share one depth map (`Interiorimg-depth.png`) and one transform, so the lens turns the same column and chair real in place. The stage enters as blank paper in the page cream and the plan draws itself on (`uReveal`: dark ink first from the vanishing point outward, then mid-tones, then colour); the rounded-card clip is gone. The lens is one formless, edgeless glow of the built room that drifts after the cursor, its shape warped by large slow noise so it never reads as a circle; double-click grows that same circle from the clicked point until it covers the stage (about 0.9 s), and shrinks it back on the next double-click. the previous frosted blob is kept in `docs/snapshots/blueprint-lens-frosted.glsl` for reverting. A liquid cursor lens reveals the built room; double-click floods it from the clicked point and back, and Blueprint / As built buttons offer the same toggle. Reduced motion holds the open stage without sway or push.
- Spiral: actual material controls for red lacquer, brushed metal and warm clay with interpolated material properties. A locally generated Three.js room environment supplies reflections; no downloaded HDRI or paid asset is involved.
- Gallery: immediate local cursor inspection rings and existing depth/parallax entrances. No global cursor replacement or delayed cursor tracking.
- Stories: shaped O aperture has directional edge shading and an expanding view; no fabricated testimonial copy was added.
- Closing scene: a lazy R3F red torus with metallic orbit geometry, pointer-responsive rotation, gentle suspended motion, a radial SVG measuring illustration, GSAP title/wordmark animation and real email CTA. Offscreen unmounting and reduced motion remain supported.

## Hero fly-through (2026-10-27, client request)

The circular portal, the morphing red form, the particle passage, the relief meshes and the low-poly animals are all retired; the original depth-map parallax rendering is back. Each hand-over is one continuous flight through a detail the two worlds share: into the rhino's eye and out of the carved eye, through the red iris into the red heart, through the blue archway into the pool's arches. The leaving world zooms ninefold on an exponential curve about its `exit` point; the next world lives inside that point as a miniature anchored on its `entry` detail, reaches full frame at 82% of the passage, and dissolves in from that detail outward along an fbm-feathered edge, with radial streaks mid-flight, a warm seam light and a slight darkening of the world being passed through.

The eye → bedroom passage opens the wall (`uPortalPass`). A bridge image, `eye-portal.jpg`, shows the same hall with the carved eye replaced by an arch of daylight and the woman far beyond; it is mapped onto `eye.jpg` by a measured fit (edge registration over the figures, foxes, platform and pillars; residual within about 3 px) in `toPortal` / `fromPortal`. Only the carved wall changes (`carvedWall`, where both depth maps agree it is wall): figures, foxes, platform and floor stay from `eye.jpg` throughout, so nothing ghosts. Over the first half the plaster cracks from the eye outward (Voronoi cells, `plasterCells`); each fragment erodes from its cracks inward, slips down with a lit rim and a thin shadow, revealing the arch; the view through the arch holds briefly; during the walk the figures and foxes defocus as a true foreground layer (`hallLayer`: each lens tap only counts what stands in front of the wall, so their colours never mix with the white wall and their edges soften over the world behind them); the walk takes the last 40%. First the carved eye crumbles away far-first along a noise front that sheds falling ivory dust, revealing the arch in place. The arch opening (`archOpening`, gated by the portal depth so the figures in front stay in front) shows the bedroom world itself from the moment the wall opens, hard-edged, never faded. Then the camera walks through the doorway: the hall and arch zoom past toward the camera (to 6.5×, with radial streaks) while the far world barely moves, drifting from the woman centred in the arch (1.1×) to its resting frame, until the arch has left the screen. The rhino-eye passage is unchanged.

The journey no longer follows scroll position frame for frame. ScrollTrigger sets a target; a ticker moves the shown progress toward it with an eased approach capped at 0.32 worlds per second (0.2 through the eye → bedroom passage), so a hard flick still plays each passage at the same pace. Titles, meters and the canvas all read the paced value. Reduced motion follows scroll directly and switches worlds without the flight.
